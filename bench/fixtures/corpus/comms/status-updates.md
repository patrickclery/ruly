# Status Updates

Status updates let people follow the work without interrupting it. Keep them short, regular and honest.

## Daily update

Post a short written update in the team channel each working day: what you finished, what you are working on and anything that blocks you. Three lines is enough. Lead with blockers, because they are the part someone else can act on.

## Weekly summary

Each project posts a weekly summary for stakeholders outside the team. It covers progress against the plan, risks and decisions needed, in that order. Use plain language and avoid internal jargon. Link to the issues rather than repeating their contents.

## Reporting risk

Report a risk as soon as you see it, not when it turns into a missed date. Say what the risk is, how likely you think it is, what it would affect and what you propose. A risk raised early is a planning input; a risk raised late is an apology.

## Incidents

During an incident, post updates at a fixed interval, even when nothing has changed. Each update says the current impact, what is being tried and when the next update will come. After the incident, link the review document in the same thread.

## Tone

Be factual. Avoid both alarm and false reassurance. If something is late, say how late and why, and give a new date only when you have a basis for it.

## Format

- Start with a one-line summary.
- Use lists for multiple items.
- Bold nothing except a blocker that needs a decision.
- Put dates in an unambiguous format with the year.

## Handover

Before time off, post a handover note on each issue you own: current state, next step and who to ask. Reassign anything that cannot wait. When you return, read the notes left for you before picking up new work.
