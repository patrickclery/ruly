# Communication

Clear writing is part of the job. Most of the team reads your work asynchronously, often hours later and without the context you had when you wrote it.

## Writing for readers who were not there

Lead with the conclusion. The first sentence of any update, comment or description should tell the reader what happened or what you need. Supporting detail follows, in order of importance.

- Name the thing: the service, the endpoint, the job, the issue key.
- State numbers with units and a time window: "p95 latency rose from 180 ms to 420 ms between 14:00 and 14:20 UTC".
- Separate facts from guesses. Mark a guess as a guess.
- Prefer short paragraphs and lists over walls of text.

## Choosing the channel

Use the issue tracker for anything that needs a record: decisions, scope changes, findings from an investigation. Use chat for quick coordination, and copy the outcome back to the issue when the conversation ends. Email is for people outside the team.

A thread that runs past twenty messages is a sign that a short call would be faster. After the call, post a three-line summary in the thread so the people who missed it can catch up.

## Asking for help

When you are stuck for more than an hour, ask. A good request for help says what you are trying to do, what you tried, what you expected and what happened instead. Include the exact error text, not a paraphrase.

## Disagreeing well

Disagree with ideas, not people. Offer an alternative when you object, and say how strongly you feel: "I would block on this" reads very differently from "small preference, take it or leave it". Once a decision is made and recorded, support it, and reopen it only with new information.

## Response times

Reply to direct questions within one working day, even if the reply is "I will look at this on Thursday". Review requests get a first response within one working day. Incidents interrupt everything else.

## Meetings

Every meeting has an agenda posted beforehand and notes posted afterward. If a meeting has no agenda, it is fine to ask for one or to decline. Decisions made in a meeting are written into the relevant issue before the end of the day.

## Written decisions

Important decisions are written down where the work lives, with the date, the people involved and the reasoning. A decision that only exists in someone's memory will be relitigated the next time it is questioned.
